# Crypto Algebraic RSA Special Primes

## When to use

Use this skill when you see:
- RSA prime generation code with matrices, quadratic forms, norms, rings, recurrences, or small random factors.
- Primes shaped like `x^2 + d*y^2`, `(x+1)^2 + d*y^2`, or norms in an imaginary quadratic order.
- Auxiliary values related to a prime are smooth even when the prime itself is large.
- Mentions of CM method, Hilbert class polynomials, elliptic curves with chosen order, Pollard p-1/p+1, Williams p+1, or ECM.

Do not use this skill when:
- RSA is vulnerable by textbook small exponent, Wiener, common modulus, partial key exposure, or padding oracle.
- There is no evidence of structured prime generation.
- Factoring by generic ECM/SIQS/NFS is expected and feasible.

## Core idea

Special RSA prime generation can accidentally give each prime an associated smooth group order. Instead of trying random curves as in generic ECM, construct or symbolically work with algebraic groups whose order is guaranteed or likely smooth modulo the hidden prime, then use a gcd during group arithmetic to split the modulus.

## Recognition signals

Look for:
- Multiplicative loops in a quadratic ring such as `Z[sqrt(-d)]`.
- Norm multiplicativity: products of small norms imply a smooth hidden value.
- Code that samples many small `a,b` pairs and multiplies matrices.
- Prime check after transforming a structured pair `(x,y)`.
- Public modulus is a product of several medium-size structured primes.
- Sage code and exact constants for `d`, class polynomials, or quadratic forms.

## Standard workflow

1. Translate prime generation into algebraic objects and identify the norm.
2. Determine which related integer is smooth because it is a product or lcm of small components.
3. Express the prime `p` and the smooth target order in terms of hidden `x,y`.
4. Choose a group whose order modulo `p` equals or divides the smooth target.
5. If roots modulo `n` are unknown, work in a quotient ring modulo `n` and the relevant polynomial.
6. Exponentiate by a multiple of the smooth target and monitor inversions/coordinates for nontrivial gcds with `n`.
7. Repeat recursively until all factors are recovered, then perform normal RSA decryption.

## Commands and tools

```bash
# Sage is the right default for quotient rings, class polynomials, and ECM-style arithmetic.
sage -python solve.sage.py

# Quick smoothness inspection for generated auxiliary values.
sage - <<'SAGE'
vals = [u^2 + 123*v^2 for u in range(1, 50) for v in range(1, 8)]
print(lcm(vals).nbits(), factor(lcm(vals)))
SAGE
```

## Pitfalls

Common ways agents get this wrong:
- Running generic factorization for hours before analyzing prime generation.
- Treating the visible prime formula as decorative instead of deriving the group order.
- Forgetting that smoothness of a related value, not `p-1`, may still enable a tailored ECM-like split.
- Needing symbolic roots modulo `n`; do not assume factoring is required before constructing the curve.
- Losing factors by not checking gcds after failed inversions or projective zero coordinates.

## Verification

Prove the solve path is real with:
- A derivation connecting prime generation to a smooth group order.
- A nontrivial gcd factor of `n` recovered by the constructed group operation.
- Full factorization and RSA plaintext recovery.
- Flag-format validation without including the flag in the skill.
